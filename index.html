Understanding the need

Problems faced by special children

Children may face barriers that affect access, belonging, confidence, and development. Families and children without parental care may experience additional challenges.

🧩

Rehabilitation and therapy

Some children need speech therapy, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, behavioural/developmental support or special education, which can be difficult for families to access consistently.

♿

Accessibility and transportation

Children with physical or sensory disabilities can face difficulties with ramps, accessible toilets, public transport and safe movement around the city.

💬

Communication

Some children have difficulty communicating their needs, understanding instructions or interacting with others, which can lead to frustration and isolation.

🤝

Bullying and exclusion

Children may be teased, bullied or excluded from games, school activities and social groups because of differences in behaviour, appearance, speech or learning.

🏡

Social judgement and care

Families can face misunderstanding or social judgement. A child’s disability may sometimes be treated as something to hide rather than as a need requiring support. Orphans, abandoned and other children in need of care may additionally face instability, lack of consistent adult support, educational disruption and difficulties accessing protection and rehabilitation services.

🌼

Confidence and well-being

Repeated academic difficulties, bullying, exclusion or lack of understanding can contribute to frustration, low confidence and withdrawal.

A different perspective

Are they really incapable?

They are not incapable. Society has often failed to recognize their potential and provide the support and opportunities they deserve.

Every child has unique strengths, talents, dreams, and ways of contributing. Some may excel academically, while others may have strengths in creativity, sports, communication, practical skills, empathy, art, or other areas.

What they need is not pity, but acceptance, encouragement, appropriate support, and genuine opportunities.

The question, therefore, is not “What can these children contribute?” but “What can we do to create an environment where their potential can be discovered and developed?”

That is the purpose behind Project Valora, to help replace exclusion with inclusion, dependency with empowerment, and overlooked potential with opportunity.

Why we exist

Aim of the project

🌱
Growing with careInclusive · Supportive · Sustainable

Project Valora aims to empower children with special needs and children without parental care through sustainable, inclusive, psychologically supportive and environmentally responsible initiatives at the local level.

By promoting equal opportunities, psychological well-being, and environmental responsibility, Project Valora strives to reduce barriers that prevent vulnerable children from reaching their full potential.

The initiative will focus on strengthening children's confidence, emotional resilience, social participation, and access to meaningful learning and development opportunities.

How we work

Our objectives

Small, thoughtful initiatives can help children participate, learn, express themselves, and feel supported.

01

Organise inclusive games, art, music, storytelling and other activities that encourage friendship and participation.

02

Educate students, families and the local community about autism, ADHD, Down syndrome, dyslexia, disabilities and the needs of children without parental care.

03

Conduct eco-friendly activities such as tree planting, recycling, upcycling and sustainable craft activities involving the children.

04

Challenge misconceptions and encourage acceptance of children with different abilities.

05

Involve teenagers and local community members in responsible, supervised volunteering.

06

Identify and understand the individual interests, strengths and needs of participating children so that Project Valora can plan activities that are appropriate and meaningful for them.

07

Create opportunities for children to develop practical life skills, such as communication, basic decision-making, teamwork, problem-solving and independence.

08

Encourage children to recognise and express their own ideas, opinions and aspirations, giving them a greater sense of confidence and ownership.

09

Provide simple learning support through age-appropriate educational resources, reading materials, games and activities where appropriate.

10

Promote emotional well-being by creating spaces where children can express their feelings, feel heard and develop healthy ways of communicating.

11

Develop low-cost and accessible resources that can be reused by schools, caregivers or community organisations to support children's learning and development.

12

Document Project Valora's activities and outcomes through photographs, reports, feedback and reflections, while protecting children's privacy and dignity.

13

Seek feedback from children, caregivers and partner organisations to understand what activities are useful and improve future initiatives.

14

Build connections with relevant local organisations and professionals who can provide guidance, expertise or referrals when a child's needs go beyond the project's capacity.

15

Promote responsible leadership among young founders by developing skills such as planning, communication, teamwork, organisation and accountability through the project.

16

Start with small, achievable initiatives and gradually expand them based on available resources, community needs and the team's capacity.

17

Maintain a child-safe approach in all Project Valora activities, ensuring appropriate adult supervision, consent, privacy and respectful treatment of participating children.

The meaning behind our name

Meaning of Valora & its connection to the aim

Valora has many meanings which includes value, courage and strength.

Value

Specially abled children and orphans are not burdens; their lives, abilities and dreams have worth.

Courage

The project helps children overcome barriers and develop confidence.

Strength

It focuses on discovering and nurturing each child’s abilities rather than focusing only on their limitations.

Opportunity

The project aims to give children access to education, activities, social inclusion and supportive environments.

Be part of the change

For volunteers

Project Valora begins with a simple belief: every child has value, potential and the right to be heard.

As volunteers, you are not expected to change the world overnight. Your time, patience, kindness and willingness to listen can make a meaningful difference in a child’s life.

By joining us, you can help create opportunities for children with special needs and children without parental care through education, creative activities, inclusion, environmental initiatives and emotional support.

We believe that change begins locally with people who care enough to take the first step.

Join Project Valora. Be a reason a child feels included, valued and empowered.
